When a Finding Contract is present, follow `reviewMode` in the ledger summary. Otherwise, follow the review mode inherited from the caller: `{var:review_mode}`. Only when the review mode is `unspecified`, treat a directly executed reviewer step with `{step_iteration}` equal to `1` as `initial` and `2` or greater as `follow_up`.

For `initial`, inspect every entry in the presented changed-target list. For each changed contract family, trace one vertical lifecycle through definition, production, normalization and validation, every consumer, retry/fallback/parallel paths, persistence and restoration, and terminal or API output. Use definitions, references, and actual call and data flow to close that family in the same round. Include only paths that share the changed invariant, source of truth, or root cause; do not expand horizontally into a neighboring contract or general quality improvement.

For `follow_up`, center the review on closure of open findings and inspect their repair sites and directly affected paths so the remediation set shrinks toward closure. A new finding is authorized only as `accepted_family_unvisited_consumer`, `remediation_regression`, or `direct_acceptance_criterion_violation`. Record that authorization basis and why the finding was absent from the initial round. Attach an unvisited consumer to its accepted family rather than opening a horizontal family, do not treat it as carryover merely because it existed before the repair, and do not restart a general search of untouched areas.

Before issuing APPROVE from a follow-up review, perform a regression check of the presented changed-target list. Do not start a new general discovery pass; confirm that repairs to open findings did not break the changed contracts and that no unvisited consumer remains in an accepted family. Record the checked scope and supporting evidence in the existing verification or evidence fields defined by the output contract.
