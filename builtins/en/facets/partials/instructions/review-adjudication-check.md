Decide each finding in the following order.

1. Separate the finding into the condition to preserve, the specific violation, and the premises needed for it to hold. Associate these with the submitted claim, failure scenario, and evidence. Identify the condition in the relevant requirements, existing contracts, and quality or design standards applicable to the finding’s perspective. Consult only the portions of those standards needed for that finding’s decision in their source files.
2. Separate matters established by the submitted evidence from undecided matters that could change acceptance. Start with the cited locations and the requirements or specifications determining their premises, and resolve undecided matters one at a time. Choose what to read according to the locations needed to resolve that uncertainty. Follow who controls values, execution authority, reachable paths, effective defenses, and claimed structures or processing volume to the locations that determine whether the claim holds. If a line number or path description is wrong, locate the claimed defect.
3. Apply established facts to the original claim and record whether the claim holds in fact, its violation of specifications, requirements, existing contracts, or quality standards, and whether it lies within the threat model, using the adjudication criteria. For improvements or proposed additions, also associate their relation to the current requirements or changes. Judge the defect separately from its proposed repair. If the threat model is not explicit, distinguish facts about actors and authority established by code or configuration from protection obligations established by requirements, existing contracts, or policies. Once acceptance is decided, record the reason and proceed to the next finding. Hand expansion of all usage sites and design of a new method for accepted problems to repair planning.
4. For accepted problems, separate the defect from the proposed repair and record the violated condition, established cause, acceptance criteria, and minimum repair boundary. When rejecting an excessive method, retain its relationship to the accepted defect and the reason. Retain each finding’s ID and source and those of its corresponding problem. For findings submitted after a repair, record the distinction and evidence if the latest repair report and verification records, the path identified by the finding, and the current code establish a repair regression.
5. For undecided items, record established facts, unresolved premises, their effect on acceptance, the next check needed, and its recipient. Separate factual investigation from requirement decisions about what must be protected.
