Decide each finding in the following order.

1. Separate the finding into the condition to preserve, the specific violation, and the premises needed for it to hold. Associate these with the submitted claim, failure scenario, and evidence. Identify the condition in the relevant requirements, existing contracts, and quality or design standards applicable to the finding’s perspective. Consult only the portions of those standards needed for that finding’s decision in their source files.
2. Separate matters established by the submitted evidence from undecided matters that could change acceptance. Start with the cited locations and the requirements or specifications determining their premises, and resolve undecided matters one at a time. Choose what to read according to the locations needed to resolve that uncertainty. Follow who controls values, execution authority, reachable paths, effective defenses, and claimed structures or processing volume to the locations that determine whether the claim holds. If a line number or path description is wrong, locate the claimed defect.
3. Apply established facts to the original claim and separately record whether it holds in fact, violates specifications, requirements, existing contracts, or quality standards, and lies within the threat model. If the threat model is not explicit, distinguish facts about actors and authority established by code or configuration from protection obligations established by requirements, existing contracts, or policies. For a proposal after repair, check whether the original acceptance criteria remain unmet or the proposal adds a distinct guarantee, input restriction, or proof method.
4. Decide separately whether repair is required in this task. Compare the protection obligation, realistic input conditions, and concrete impact if left unresolved with restrictions, complexity, and regressions to normal use introduced by repair. If a supplied human decision rejected a guarantee, associate its content, reason, and scope, and do not overturn it through rewording. If new evidence invalidates its premises, identify the difference and the requirement decision needed. Do not infer an unsupplied human decision. Record repair, carry-forward, or outside-requirements treatment and the reason; pass changes in obligations or scope to replanning.
5. For accepted problems, separate the defect from the proposed repair and record the violated condition, established cause, acceptance criteria, and minimum repair boundary. When rejecting an excessive method, retain its relationship to the accepted defect and the reason. Retain each finding’s ID and source and those of its corresponding problem. For findings submitted after a repair, record the distinction and evidence if the latest repair report and verification records, the path identified by the finding, and the current code establish a repair regression. Hand expansion of all usage sites and design of a new method for accepted problems to repair planning.
6. For undecided items, record established facts, unresolved premises, their effect on acceptance, the next check needed, and its recipient. Separate factual investigation from requirement decisions about what must be protected.
